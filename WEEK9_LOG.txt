WEEK 9 — DONE (facility-set expansion for Track B)

- RESEARCH_PLAN.md Sec 15 named facility-set expansion as the next step
  once Week 8's per-overpass wind fix was validated. This week does that:
  went from 4 processed plants (2/4 plausible) to 9 (7/9 producing an
  estimate), targeting RESEARCH_PLAN.md's 10-20-plant goal but stopping
  short of it once the real per-plant processing cost became clear (see
  "Processing cost" below).

- pick_plants.py: extended from top-5 to top-20 India coal plants by
  capacity (data/powerplants.csv). Added dedup: the raw dataset lists
  separate generating units at the same site under different names (e.g.
  "MUNDRA TPP" and "MUNDRA UMPP" ~0.05 deg apart) -- units within ~5km of
  an already-kept, higher-capacity unit are now collapsed into one
  candidate. The 4 plants already processed in Weeks 6-8 (Vindhyachal,
  Sasan, Mundra, Tirora) keep their existing short aliases so
  process_plant.py's registry generalization (below) doesn't touch their
  already-saved data. Output: data/candidate_plants.csv (20 candidates).

- check_coverage.py (new): metadata-only earthaccess.search_data() call
  per candidate (no download) to rank/pre-filter before the expensive
  per-plant scan. Result: all 20 candidates returned the identical
  granule count (360) for the same year/bounding-box search. This is a
  negative result worth recording -- granule-count pre-filtering at this
  bounding-box scale does not discriminate at all; actual near-plant hit
  density (the number that matters) only shows up after the full
  per-sounding scan, same as the Tirora/Mundra thin-coverage findings
  from Weeks 6-8. The pre-check script is kept for future use but isn't a
  useful filter as currently written -- a real filter would need to
  narrow the bounding box or check actual near-plant proximity per
  granule, which is most of the cost of process_plant.py itself.

- process_plant.py: PLANTS registry generalized from a hardcoded 4-entry
  dict to a load from data/candidate_plants.csv, so any candidate can be
  processed via `python process_plant.py <Name>` without further code
  changes. Verified the 4 existing plants' lat/lon in the CSV are within
  ~0.002 deg of their old hardcoded values (rounding only, no effective
  change to near/background sounding membership).

- physics_ime.py: removed the stale NPZ_PATHS override
  ({"Vindhyachal": "data/vindhyachal_soundings.npz"}), a leftover from
  before the estimate loop was generalized to read data/plant_results.json
  directly -- the actual file is data/Vindhyachal_soundings.npz, so the
  override was dead code that happened not to matter (the fallback
  pattern f"data/{name}_soundings.npz" already resolves correctly).

- Processed 5 new plants this week (Talcher, Rihand, Sipat, ChandrapurCoal,
  Anpara), chosen as the 4 highest-capacity new candidates plus Talcher
  (the first one tried, used to establish per-plant timing) -- user opted
  to stop at 9 total plants rather than the full 20-candidate list once
  the ~50-65 min/plant cost was measured (see below).

  Talcher    21 hit-days, 2165 soundings, +0.24 ppm enhancement,
             NO2 peak 35km away, wind/CO2 offset diff 25 deg (good
             alignment) -> Q = 7.16M +/- 1.65M t/yr [5.51M, 8.82M]
             (tightest relative uncertainty of any plant this week --
             20% wind term vs. 45-74% for the others)

  Rihand     16 hit-days, 6072 soundings, +1.89 ppm enhancement (largest
             signal of any plant processed to date), NO2 peak only 8km
             away (best co-location), wind/CO2 offset diff 109 deg (poor
             alignment) -> Q = 47.47M +/- 35.15M t/yr [12.32M, 82.62M]
             (74% wind relative std -- the least precise estimate this
             week, driven by only 4 matched wind days with high day-to-
             day spread)

  Sipat      1 hit-day, 63 soundings, NaN enhancement (all 63 soundings
             landed in the near-plant radius, none in the background
             annulus, so bg.mean() was undefined), NO2 peak 55km away,
             wind/CO2 offset diff 101 deg -> skipped by physics_ime.py
             (near=0 after the near/bg split used there). Effectively
             unusable, same outcome class as Mundra.

  ChandrapurCoal  14 hit-days, 1136 soundings, +0.65 ppm enhancement, NO2
             peak only 2km away (best co-location of the week), wind/CO2
             offset diff 177 deg -- essentially opposite directions, the
             worst alignment seen on any plant to date -> Q = 11.78M +/-
             5.82M t/yr [5.96M, 17.59M]. Fell back to annual-mean wind
             mode (0 days met the per-overpass match threshold) despite
             having 28 near-plant soundings used, worth a closer look in
             a future session -- the wind/CO2 misalignment and the
             fallback-mode wind result may be related.

  Anpara     16 hit-days, 5768 soundings, +1.26 ppm enhancement, NO2 peak
             17km away, wind/CO2 offset diff 80 deg (moderate) ->
             Q = 31.66M +/- 21.43M t/yr [10.23M, 53.09M]

- Full 9-plant emission_estimates.json after physics_ime.py rerun:
  Sasan 39.96M +/- 17.90M, Tirora 3.22M +/- 1.70M, Vindhyachal 36.87M +/-
  21.95M (all three unchanged from Week 8 -- confirms the registry
  generalization didn't perturb the original 4 plants' results), plus the
  5 new plants above. Mundra and Sipat skipped (0 near-plant soundings
  after the near/bg split).

- Plausibility comparison: Week 8 baseline was 2/4 processed plants
  giving a physically plausible estimate (Sasan, Vindhyachal; Tirora too
  low; Mundra skipped). This week: 7/9 processed plants produce an
  estimate at all (up from 3/4), and of the 5 new plants, 4/5 produce a
  magnitude in the same broad range as the two originally-plausible
  plants (7-47M t/yr) -- Sipat is the one clear non-starter. The wider
  spread and generally larger relative uncertainty (45-74% wind term vs.
  Talcher's 20%) suggests plausibility by magnitude alone is a weak
  signal here; the wind/CO2 offset diff (25-177 deg across the 5 new
  plants) looks like a more informative per-plant quality flag than
  hit-day count or soundings count, worth formalizing as an explicit
  filter/warning in a future pass rather than just printing it.

- Processing cost: confirmed ~50-65 min per plant end-to-end (dominated
  by the 360-granule/year OCO-3 scan, which is network-bound, not
  CPU-bound). At this rate, all 20 shortlisted candidates would take
  13-15 hours unattended. User chose to process 5 new plants (9 total)
  this session rather than the full list -- this is now a documented,
  measured constraint on facility-set expansion, not a guess.

- Operational notes (checkpointing from Week 8, exercised under real
  conditions this week):
  * Rihand and Sipat each had one slow/large-granule download (one
    granule took several minutes instead of the usual seconds) that
    looked like a stall at first glance -- confirmed via the partial
    file's growing size in data/scan_tmp/, not an actual hang. No
    intervention needed; both recovered on their own.
  * ChandrapurCoal's background-task tracker reported "failed" (exit
    code 144) partway through its scan while the actual OS process was
    still alive and continued running to completion normally -- a
    monitoring/tooling artifact, not a pipeline bug. Verified via direct
    process/checkpoint inspection rather than trusting the tracker.
  * Anpara was genuinely interrupted once at granule 294/360 (process
    actually gone, resource_tracker semaphore-leak warning in the log
    consistent with a hard kill). Restarting `python process_plant.py
    Anpara` resumed cleanly from the checkpoint with zero re-download and
    finished normally. This is exactly the failure mode the Week 8
    checkpointing was added for, and it worked as designed.
