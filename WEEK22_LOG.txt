Week 21's real-tile sanity check (WEEK21_LOG.txt's qualitative addendum)
found the segmentation model's predicted "plume" pixels tracked real

STEP 4: RE-RUN THE REAL-TILE QUALITATIVE CHECK -- THE ACTUAL TEST
=====================================================================
Same two facilities as Week 21 (Vindhyachal, Rihand), same script
(sanity_check_real_tiles.py), same single best-covered real overpass day
per plant (2020-11-30, both), retrained checkpoint. Same hard
constraint: no accuracy metric computed against real tiles -- there is
still no real ground-truth mask to score against.

RESULT: the coverage-tracking behavior is NOT fixed. Visually
(data/unet_segmentation_real_tile_sanity_check.png, regenerated): the
predicted "plume" pixels for both plants again cluster in a rough
diagonal band that closely resembles the shape of the real data's own
denser coverage sub-region, and that band again looks similar between
the two plants (same day, geographically close, so their coverage
speckle looks alike) -- the same qualitative pattern Week 21 found,
not visibly more plume-shaped. Predicted plume-pixel fraction changed
slightly (0.5%->1.1% for Vindhyachal, 0.6%->2.0% for Rihand) but the
SHAPE of where those predictions land did not become more plausible as
a plume: still a scattered cluster tracking coverage structure, not an
elongated streak.

This is a materially different outcome than the simulated-data numbers
suggest on their own -- exactly the risk this task's own instructions
flagged going in ("the simulated metric passing is necessary but not
sufficient evidence the underlying problem is fixed"). Density was
fixed and confirmed (Step 1); the coverage-PATTERN-tracking behavior
this whole week targeted was not resolved by that fix alone.

DIAGNOSIS, not just the observation: matching coverage DENSITY was not
sufficient because the coverage-tracking behavior is not primarily a
density problem -- it is a STRUCTURE problem. The model appears to have
learned an association between the SHAPE/geometry of the valid-vs-
invalid pixel boundary and "plume," largely independent of the actual
XCO2 values within the valid region, and simply making that boundary
sparser (rather than changing its geometric character) does not remove
that association. Two candidate contributing factors, neither confirmed
this session (flagged for whoever continues this): (a) the fixed
NaN-fill value (BG_XCO2_PPM=415.0) creates a sharp discontinuity at
every valid/invalid boundary that a small conv-net can trivially detect
regardless of density, giving it a shortcut feature correlated with
SOMETHING in training (facility identity via retention_frac, day-to-day
geometry, etc.) that generalizes poorly; (b) even now, this project's
own simulated SAM footprint pattern is a fixed local (east, north)
raster (no simulated satellite heading, a documented simplification
since Task 6) cropped to a square tile, which may still be
structurally different from real coverage speckle's actual shape/
texture even at matched density -- density and shape are not the same
property, and this week only fixed the former.

VERDICT: SECOND CONFIRMED NEGATIVE RESULT on the same underlying
transfer-to-real-data problem (Week 21's first attempt, this week's
second). Per explicit instruction, applying the same diminishing-
returns discipline as Week 20: reported honestly, NOT forcing a third
variant in this session. This now looks like a MORE FUNDAMENTAL sim-
to-real gap than a single fixable coverage-density parameter -- fixing
density alone was not enough, and the remaining gap (coverage-mask
SHAPE/structure, and/or a NaN-fill-boundary shortcut-learning problem)
is not obviously a one-line fix the way the density mismatch was. This
echoes, and now provides second-hand evidence for, the open question
already recorded in SIMULATOR_METHODOLOGY_NOTE.md Sec 6 item 2 ("a
different label-generation methodology entirely") -- two independent
attempts (multi-day pooling in Week 20 Task 5, and now geometry-only
coverage matching here) at making a single-snapshot forward-simulated
tile behave like real satellite data have both fallen short in ways
that point toward the SIMULATION METHODOLOGY itself, not a tunable
parameter within it.

NOT attempted this session, listed so they aren't re-derived from
scratch: (a) diagnosing whether the NaN-fill-boundary shortcut-learning
hypothesis above is real (e.g. training with a second input channel
encoding validity explicitly, rather than relying on a boundary
discontinuity implicitly, as Week 21's original "either mask out of the
loss function or treat as a documented input channel" choice already
flagged as the alternative not taken); (b) modeling real satellite
ground-track heading/orbit geometry rather than the fixed local-frame
SAM raster, to test whether coverage SHAPE (not just density) is the
missing piece; (c) abandoning single-snapshot simulated tiles as the
segmentation training source entirely, per SIMULATOR_METHODOLOGY_NOTE.md
Sec 6 item 2.
