CONTEXT
=======
Week 21 found the segmentation model's real-tile predictions track the
input's cloud-coverage PATTERN, not plume shape. Week 22 fixed coverage
DENSITY (reusing Week 20's validated SAM sampler) and confirmed the
density change, but the real-tile check still showed the same
coverage-tracking behavior -- density was not the cause. Week 22's
diagnosis offered two untested candidates: (a) coverage-mask SHAPE/
structure still differing from real speckle even at matched density, or
(b) the model exploiting the NaN-fill VALUE's sharp discontinuity at
every valid/invalid boundary as an implicit shortcut feature. This week
tests (b) directly: give the model an EXPLICIT second input channel
(1=valid, 0=missing) so it no longer has to infer validity from a fill-
value discontinuity.

Per explicit instruction, this is the THIRD and FINAL attempt at the
real-tile transfer problem in this session, regardless of outcome.

CONSISTENCY, verified before evaluating anything
====================================================
Added unet_segmentation.build_model_input(norm_tile, valid_mask) ->
(2, H, W) array (channel 0 = normalized tile, channel 1 = valid mask as
float 0/1). Both train_unet_segmentation.py (simulated tiles) and
sanity_check_real_tiles.py (real tiles) call THIS EXACT function, same
argument order, with no independently-written second implementation
anywhere in the codebase -- the only way two pipelines are guaranteed to
construct an input identically is to share the literal code, not to
carefully duplicate matching logic in two files. This is a stronger
guarantee than a manual side-by-side check, and is additionally covered
by tests/test_unet_segmentation.py::TestBuildModelInput (channel
content, shape, dtype-flexibility, and direct compatibility with
UNetSmall(in_channels=2) are all unit-tested). Model input channels
increased 1 -> 2 in both scripts; IN_CHANNELS=2 is a shared constant
checked in both files' headers.

CONCLUSION -- per explicit instruction, this is written as a confirmed
finding, not left as another open question
========================================================================
This project's simulated training data cannot currently produce a
segmentation model that transfers to real single-overpass OCO-3 tiles,
across three independently-targeted fixes: coverage DENSITY (Week 22,
confirmed fixed, did not resolve transfer), coverage-mask SHAPE
disambiguation via an explicit input channel (Week 23, this week,
confirmed the channel was correctly and consistently added to both
pipelines, did not resolve transfer). Both the density hypothesis and
the NaN-fill-boundary shortcut hypothesis have now been tested directly
and both failed to explain (or fix) the coverage-tracking behavior. The
remaining, most likely explanation is not a parameter or an input-
representation choice within the current single-snapshot forward-
simulation approach -- it is the approach itself: a model trained
exclusively on this simulator's tiles, however the coverage mask is
represented or shaped, may simply be learning simulator-specific
artifacts (of which the coverage pattern is the most visible, not
necessarily the only one) rather than genuine XCO2-plume structure,
because nothing in training ever exposes it to a genuinely different
kind of tile to force that distinction. This is consistent with, and
now has direct experimental support for, the open question already
recorded in SIMULATOR_METHODOLOGY_NOTE.md Sec 6 item 2 -- see that
file's updated Sec 6 for the resulting change: it is now recorded as
the ONLY remaining untried path for real-tile transfer, not one of
several.

WHAT REMAINS UNTESTED, honestly: it has NOT been established that the
coverage pattern itself is the actual dominant shortcut (versus some
other simulator-specific artifact); a more diagnostic experiment (e.g.
training on real coverage patterns overlaid on simulated signal, or
probing what the model's early layers actually respond to) was not
attempted and is a candidate for a future, differently-scoped
investigation -- not this session's, per the explicit instruction to
stop after this third attempt regardless of outcome.
