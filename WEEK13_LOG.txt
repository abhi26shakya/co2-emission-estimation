WEEK 13 — experiment 1: overpass-density subsampling (mixed result --
                         density matters, but is not the whole story)

- Question from Week 12's close: is the limiting factor on satellite-only
  Q estimation simply OCO-3 revisit frequency? Vindhyachal (17 overpass
  days, our best-covered facility) gives the best result in the N=24
  table (Q 36.7 Mt vs CEA 33.2 Mt, +11%); most other plants have 4-7 days
  and fail badly. Hypothesis: day count alone explains the spread.

- overpass_density_experiment.py (new): for Vindhyachal, Rihand, and
  Sasan (the three best-covered facilities, 16-19 overpass days each),
  randomly subsampled down to n_days in [17,15,12,10,8,6,5,4,3], 200
  repeats per (plant, n_days), seed 42. Each repeat restricts BOTH the
  near-plant and background sounding pools to only the sampled days
  (matching what actually having fewer overpasses would look like), then
  reruns the existing IME estimator unchanged --
  physics_gaussian.estimate_emission_rate_from_arrays(), a new function
  factored out of estimate_emission_rate() so the math is reused exactly,
  not reimplemented. Verified the refactor is behavior-preserving before
  running anything: full-coverage output for all three plants reproduced
  data/emission_estimates.json's Q to 6 significant figures.

- Result, Vindhyachal (full coverage +11% vs CEA):
  n_days:        17    15    12    10    8     6     5     4     3
  error vs CEA: +11%  +10%   +3%  -17%  -25%  -42%  -47%  -49%  -56%
  spread(std):   0%   16%   28%   38%   49%   67%   79%   81%   84%
  Smooth, monotonic degradation in both bias and spread as days drop.
  This plant supports the hypothesis cleanly.

- Result, Sasan (full coverage +37% vs CEA):
  n_days:        17    15    12    10    8     6     5     4     3
  error vs CEA: +37%  +33%  +14%  -19%  -31%  -41%  -54%  -61%  -64%
  Same monotonic pattern, just centered on a worse full-coverage bias.

- Result, Rihand (full coverage +134% vs CEA) -- the negative finding:
  n_days:        15    12    10    8     6     5     4     3
  error vs CEA: +134% +122% +137% +69%  +38%  +30%  +5%   -10%
  Rihand's estimate is already 2.3x too high at its BEST available
  coverage (16 days -> 15 usable after month-stratification). More days
  did not fix it; it only ever oscillated around a bad number. Its
  apparent "improvement" at n_days=4-5 is noise, not a real trend -- see
  below.

- Applied a threshold definition and caught it being fooled by that
  noise: the first version scanned ascending n_days and returned the
  smallest n_days where |error|<=50% -- this reported Rihand as "stable"
  at n_days=4 (+5% error, a lucky draw) despite n_days=5 and 6 failing
  the same test on either side of it. Fixed to a descending scan that
  requires an UNBROKEN stable run down from full coverage, stopping at
  the first failure. Re-run with the fix: Rihand's threshold is None (its
  own full-coverage point never clears 50% error), Vindhyachal=4,
  Sasan=6. This kind of self-caught methodology bug is exactly the thing
  worth logging, per this repo's honesty convention.

- VERDICT: overpass density is A real factor (Vindhyachal and Sasan both
  degrade smoothly and predictably as days are removed) but NOT THE ONLY
  factor and not sufficient to explain the pattern across facilities --
  Rihand fails at its best achievable coverage, meaning something else
  (background definition, wind matching, plume geometry, real emission
  variability) dominates its error independent of day count. Day count
  can turn a good facility bad (more noise, no signal to lose) but cannot
  turn a facility with a bad underlying signal good.

  Practical read: adopting the (conservative, max-across-plants) n_days>=6
  threshold, 17 of the 24 plants with both a Q estimate and CEA truth
  clear it (data/q_correction_model_results.json). That is NOT the same
  17 that pass the Week 11 quality gate (hit_days>=10 AND
  wind_co2_diff_deg<=60, N=7) -- density alone is a looser, weaker filter
  than the existing gate, consistent with Week 12's finding that the gate
  is doing *something* even though it didn't survive permutation testing.

Files changed: physics_gaussian.py (estimate_emission_rate() split into a
loader + new estimate_emission_rate_from_arrays(), behavior-preserving,
verified against data/emission_estimates.json before use),
overpass_density_experiment.py (new), data/overpass_density_results.json
(new), data/overpass_density_plot.png (new),
data/{Vindhyachal,Rihand,Sasan}_wind_series_cache.json (new, full-year
ERA5 daily wind speed per plant, cached to avoid re-hitting Earth Engine
on reruns).

WEEK 13 — experiment 2: signal-to-noise across all 30 plants (negative
                         result on both questions asked)

- diagnose_talcher.py (Week 10) computed signal-to-noise -- (near-plant
  mean - background mean) / background std -- for exactly two plants:
  Talcher (0.18, a known-bad case) and Rihand (1.27, used there as the
  "robust" comparison plant). Two open questions from Week 12/13 exp 1
  motivated generalizing it to all 30: (1) is SNR a better predictor of
  |log_ratio| than hit_days (r=-0.31, Week 12)? (2) does Rihand's Week 13
  exp-1 failure (bad Q even at full coverage) trace to a low SNR that
  day-count alone couldn't see?

- snr_all_plants.py (new): reuses diagnose_talcher.near_bg_stats()
  unchanged across all 30 plants (6 skipped for too few near/bg
  soundings: Akaltara, Bellary, Lalitpur, Mundra, Simhadri, Sipat).
  Matched to the N=24 feature table (plants with both a Q estimate and
  CEA truth) for the correlation test.

- Q1 answer: NO, SNR is not a better predictor -- and its sign is the
  wrong direction. r(SNR, |log_ratio|) = +0.27 vs r(hit_days,
  |log_ratio|) = -0.31 (Week 12, reproduced exactly here). hit_days wins
  on magnitude, and SNR's positive sign is backwards from the physical
  expectation (higher SNR should mean a cleaner signal and lower error,
  not higher). Scatter (data/snr_vs_log_ratio_plot.png) shows no visible
  relationship -- points span the full |log_ratio| range at every SNR
  level.

- Q2 answer: NO. Rihand's SNR = 1.27, well ABOVE the 30-plant median
  (0.36) and Talcher's 0.18 -- Rihand has one of the cleanest raw signals
  in the dataset, not a marginal one. Its Week 13 exp-1 failure (+134%
  vs CEA at full coverage) is NOT a signal-to-noise problem. Ruling this
  out narrows the real cause further: not day count (exp 1), not signal
  strength (exp 2). Remaining untested candidates: background-annulus
  definition sensitivity, wind-day matching quality, plume
  geometry/plant layout, or a genuine CEA-vs-2020-satellite-year
  emissions mismatch specific to Rihand.

- VERDICT: two negative results. SNR does not explain the quality-gate
  pattern better than hit_days already did, and it does not explain
  Rihand specifically. Consistent with this repo's convention of
  reporting negative results in full, not just the ones that pan out.

Files changed (experiment 2): snr_all_plants.py (new),
data/snr_all_plants.json (new), data/snr_vs_log_ratio_plot.png (new). No
existing files modified.

WEEK 13 — experiment 3: background-definition sensitivity across all 30
                         plants (Rihand ruled out a third time)

- Week 10's diagnose_talcher.py tested this for exactly two plants:
  Talcher's IME estimate swung 20.2% across 5 reasonable
  background-annulus definitions, Rihand's only 4.3%. Generalized to all
  30 plants to (a) confirm Rihand's swing is still small once tested
  against the full dataset, not just the original 2-plant comparison,
  and (b) check whether swing% predicts |log_ratio| better than hit_days
  (r=-0.31) or SNR (r=+0.27, experiment 2).

- bg_sensitivity_all_plants.py (new): calls
  diagnose_talcher.bg_definition_sensitivity() UNMODIFIED for all 30
  plants (4 skipped for too few background soundings in any definition:
  Bellary, Lalitpur; 5 more report swing=n/a because their default-
  definition IME proxy is exactly 0 -- Akaltara, Mundra, Simhadri, Sipat,
  TalwandiSabo -- an existing, already-documented behavior of the reused
  function, not something this script had to handle specially).

- Rihand check: swing = 4.3% in this 30-plant run, EXACTLY matching
  Week 10's 4.3% (same computation, same inputs -- expected, but worth
  confirming nothing shifted). Still far below Talcher's 20.2%
  known-bad reference.

- Predictor comparison (N=23, matched to the feature table, excluding
  n/a swings): r(swing%, |log_ratio|) = +0.16, weaker than both hit_days
  (-0.31) and SNR (+0.27, experiment 2). Ranked by |r|: hit_days (0.31)
  > SNR (0.27) > swing% (0.16). A third predictor, a third negative
  result -- none of hit_days, SNR, or background-definition swing is a
  strong standalone predictor of |log_ratio| across the dataset.

- VERDICT: background-annulus definition is RULED OUT as Rihand's
  specific problem, same as overpass coverage (exp 1) and
  signal-to-noise (exp 2) were. Three independent explanations have now
  been tested and rejected for Rihand's error. What's left untested:
  wind-day matching quality (Rihand's per-overpass wind match rate was
  low -- 4-5 of 16 days in data/emission_estimates.json) and plume
  geometry/plant layout. Also noted in passing, not investigated further
  this week: Sagardighi's swing is a 866% outlier, the largest in the
  dataset by a wide margin -- a candidate for its own diagnose_*.py-style
  deep dive later, same pattern as Talcher's Week 10 investigation.

Files changed (experiment 3): bg_sensitivity_all_plants.py (new),
data/bg_sensitivity_all_plants.json (new). No existing files modified.

WEEK 13 — experiment 4: wind-matching quality across all 30 plants
                         (strongest predictor found, but still doesn't
                         explain Rihand)

- physics_gaussian.py's n_wind_days_matched is threshold-gated: a plant
  with 1-2 real per-overpass wind matches (below MIN_WIND_DAYS_MATCHED=3)
  still reports n_wind_days_matched=0, since the code falls back to the
  annual-mean speed for the whole plant. That flattens 15 of 24 plants
  to an artificial 0, masking real variation. Added two purely additive
  fields to estimate_emission_rate_from_arrays() -- n_wind_days_raw_matched
  (the true, unthresholded count) and wind_days_matched_speeds (the
  actual per-day speeds found, even below threshold) -- verified
  behavior-preserving against data/emission_estimates.json before use
  (no existing keys changed).

- wind_match_quality_all_plants.py (new): recomputes wind_match_rate =
  n_wind_days_raw_matched / hit_days for all 30 plants (6 skipped as in
  prior experiments for too few near/bg soundings: Akaltara, Bellary,
  Lalitpur, Simhadri, Sipat; Mundra additionally has no per-sounding day
  field at all). For the 15 fallback-mode plants, also computed how far
  the annual-mean speed actually used deviates from the real per-day
  speeds that were found but discarded by the threshold -- median swing
  in the 20-60% range per plant, confirming the fallback approximation
  really is a meaningful source of error where it applies, independent
  of what explains Rihand specifically.

- Rihand check: wind_match_rate = 25.0% (4/16 days), confirming
  experiment 3's ~25-31% estimate exactly. Rank 17th of 24 computable
  plants, 71st percentile -- ABOVE MEDIAN, not a low outlier. Rihand's
  wind matching is better than most of the dataset.

- Predictor comparison (N=24): r(wind_match_rate, |log_ratio|) = -0.328,
  the strongest of the four tested so far -- edging out hit_days (-0.310),
  SNR (+0.269, exp 2), and bg swing% (+0.161, exp 3). But "strongest"
  here means barely: -0.328 vs -0.310 is a marginal difference, and
  |r|=0.33 still only explains ~11% of the variance in |log_ratio|. None
  of the four predictors tested this week is strong in an absolute sense.

- VERDICT: wind-matching quality is technically the best single predictor
  found across all four Week 13 experiments, but it does NOT explain
  Rihand -- Rihand sits in the upper-mid range on this metric (71st
  percentile) despite one of the worst Q errors in the dataset (+134%).
  Four independent candidate explanations for Rihand specifically have
  now been tested and rejected: overpass density (exp 1), signal-to-noise
  (exp 2), background definition (exp 3), and wind-matching quality
  (exp 4). None of the four single-cause hypotheses explains it. Whatever
  is wrong with Rihand is either a genuine plume-geometry/plant-layout
  effect, a real FY2020-21-CEA-vs-2020-satellite emissions mismatch
  specific to that plant, or an interaction between factors rather than
  any one of them alone -- none of which this week's four
  single-variable experiments were designed to catch.
