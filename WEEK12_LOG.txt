                 results negative)

  "hit_days >= 10 AND wind_co2_diff_deg <= 60" quality gate (N=7, bias
  1.07x, sd(log) 0.55, within-2x 6/7) a real finding, or did we fit it to
  the same 24 points we then judged it on? Started this week by
  re-verifying CLAUDE.md's headline numbers against
  data/q_correction_model_results.json directly -- all of them (median
  bias 0.69x, sd(log) 1.33, within-2x 10/24, r(log our_q, CEA)=0.236,
  r(log capacity_mw, CEA)=0.776, and the filtered N=7 numbers above)
  reproduced exactly. Ground was solid; the open question was not a
  data-entry error, it needed an actual validation script.

- baseline_capacity.py (new): before touching the gate question, asked
  the more basic one CLAUDE.md's own correlation numbers implied --
  does capacity_mw alone predict CEA better than our satellite estimate?
  Four predictors, all evaluated leave-one-out on the N=24 table so
  nothing is fit and scored on the same point:

    A. capacity_mw only (N=24):        LOO R^2 = 0.527, MAE(log) = 0.398,
                                        within-2x 20/24
    B. IME Q, ungated (N=24):          LOO R^2 = -0.152, MAE(log) = 0.596,
                                        within-2x 15/24
    C. IME Q, gated only (N=7):        LOO R^2 = -0.242, MAE(log) = 0.517,
                                        within-2x 5/7
    D. capacity_mw + IME Q combined:   LOO R^2 = 0.505, MAE(log) = 0.404,
                                        within-2x 20/24

  Capacity alone wins outright. Both satellite predictors have NEGATIVE
  LOO R^2 -- worse than predicting the mean. Combining capacity with IME Q
  (D) is not better than capacity alone (0.505 vs 0.527 R^2, 0.404 vs
  0.398 MAE) -- the satellite signal adds nothing once capacity is in the
  model. Reported plainly, not spun: the defensible claim for continuing
  satellite work is not "more accurate than capacity," it currently is
  not. It's that capacity_mw is nameplate, not actual output, and
  satellite needs no self-reported operator data -- a scope argument, not
  an accuracy one.

- Restricted comparison (same reviewer objection: comparing C's N=7
  against A/B/D's N=24 isn't apples-to-apples). Re-ran capacity-only and
  capacity+IME restricted to the identical 7 gated plants, LOO within that
  subset:

    E. capacity_mw only, gated subset (N=7):  MAE(log) = 0.241, R^2=0.665,
                                               within-2x 6/7
    C. IME Q, gated subset (N=7):             MAE(log) = 0.517, R^2=-0.242,
                                               within-2x 5/7
    F. capacity+IME combined, gated (N=7):    MAE(log) = 0.350, R^2=0.311,
                                               within-2x 5/7

  Capacity still wins on the gated subset, and not narrowly (0.241 vs
  0.517). Key insight, and the one that reframes the gate question:
  capacity ALSO improves sharply on the gated subset (all-24 MAE 0.398 ->
  gated-only 0.241), while capacity restricted to the 17 NON-gated plants
  stays close to its full-sample number (0.418). That pattern means the
  "quality gate" is plausibly selecting plants that are generally easier
  to predict -- for any predictor -- not plants where the satellite
  estimate specifically becomes trustworthy relative to capacity. This
  motivated treating the gate itself as suspect, not just under-tested.

- validate_quality_gate.py (new) + tests/test_validate_quality_gate.py
  (9 tests, all passing): three checks against the N=24 table, seed 42,
  MIN_GATE_N=5 (minimum plants a candidate gate must keep to be
  considered, needed since sd() requires n>=2 and to keep candidate gates
  roughly the scale of the published N=7 gate).

  1. Leave-one-facility-out: for each of 24 facilities, select the best
     (hit_days, wind_diff) threshold on the OTHER 23 only (minimizing
     sd(log_ratio) among passing plants), then apply it to the held-out
     plant. Gated held-out plants (n=6): sd(log_ratio)=0.595, within-2x
     5/6. Ungated held-out plants (n=18): sd(log_ratio)=1.486, within-2x
     5/18. Real, out-of-fold improvement for gated plants -- the gate
     is not nothing.

  2. Permutation test (the decisive one): shuffled log_ratio labels
     across facilities 1000 times (hit_days/wind_diff held fixed), re-ran
     the same best-gate search on each shuffle. 234/1000 shuffles matched
     or beat the real grid-search-optimal gate's sd (0.554). p = 0.235,
     nowhere near the p<0.05 bar. Random label-scrambled data finds an
     equally good-looking gate about 1 time in 4 -- the signature of a
     multiple-comparisons artifact, not a real threshold effect.

  3. Threshold stability: 0/24 LOO folds reproduced the published (10,60)
     exactly. The search converged on (9, 54.0) in 22/24 folds instead
     (and, incidentally, (9,54.0) on the full data selects the identical
     7 plants (10,60) does -- the induced subset is stable even though
     the literal threshold numbers the search finds are not what's
     published). That the grid-search optimum doesn't reproduce the
     published numbers at all is itself worth flagging.

  Per-fold detail: of the 24 held-out plants, 17 had |log_ratio| below
  the ungated-group average (1.226) -- including 10 that FAILED the gate
  (e.g. Kudgi 0.241, Koradi 0.324, Raichur 0.426). Most plants in this
  dataset are just easy; the gate's apparent power partly reflects that
  base rate rather than a clean separation of easy vs. hard cases.

  VERDICT: GATE IS SELECTION BIAS. Per the pre-agreed criteria
  (permutation p<0.05 AND LOO shows real gated-vs-ungated improvement),
  the permutation leg fails even though the LOO leg alone looked
  supportive. Report the honest nuance, not just the verdict label: gated
  plants DID show real out-of-fold improvement (sd 0.595 vs 1.486) --
  the gate is doing something -- it just isn't doing more than what
  label-shuffled noise does about a quarter of the time, which is not
  a result you can build a correction model's credibility on.

- Combined picture across all three results this week: capacity beats
  satellite everywhere it was tested (full sample, gated subset, and
  combined with satellite), and the gate that was supposed to identify
  where satellite becomes trustworthy does not survive a permutation
  test. All three are negative results and are recorded as such, per
  this repo's convention (VIIRS tying exactly, activity signal not
  helping, reliability model LOO R^2 = -1.22 -- this is consistent with
  that pattern, not an aberration).

NEXT: the research question has shifted. It is no longer "how do we make
this work" -- three independent tests this week say the current N=24
satellite-only approach does not beat a trivial nameplate-capacity
baseline, gated or not. The question is now "why doesn't it, and what
satellite coverage would be required for it to." Week 13 will run an
overpass-density experiment on Vindhyachal (our best-covered facility,
n_soundings=7114, hit_days=17) to test whether the limiting factor is
OCO-3 revisit frequency itself -- i.e. whether Q-estimate precision
scales with sounding density in a way that predicts how many more
overpasses the other 23 facilities would need before satellite-only
Q estimation could plausibly compete with capacity.
