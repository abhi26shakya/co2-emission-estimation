WEEK 7 — DONE (highway hard-negative expansion)

- Week 5 left highways as the top non-steel false-alarm source
  (hwy_AhmedabadVadodara 0.46, hwy_GTRoad_UP 0.44), while VIIRS
  successfully reduced steel-plant confidence (Bhilai, Jamshedpur).
  Hypothesis: the existing 5 highway hard negatives were too narrow a
  sample (concentrated in north/west India) for the model to learn a
  general "linear NO2/SO2 corridor != combustion source" boundary, so
  it was overfitting to those specific corridors' NO2/SO2/thermal
  signatures rather than the shape/pattern that makes them non-plants.
- Added 5 new highway hard negatives, chosen to spread geographically
  rather than cluster near the existing worst offenders (see
  data/hard_negatives.csv): hwy_BangaloreChennai, hwy_KolkataDurgapur,
  hwy_JaipurAjmer, hwy_PuneBangalore, hwy_LucknowKanpur.
- Ran export_hard_negatives.py -> export_so2.py -> export_viirs.py
  (resume-friendly, only fetched the 5 new locations x 24 months x 3
  channels = 360 new tiles; existing 15 locations' tiles untouched),
  then build_3channel.py (hard_negative count 360 -> 480), then
  train_3channel.py.

CONCLUSION: the hypothesis held. Both of Week 5's worst highway false
alarms dropped meaningfully once the model saw a geographically
broader sample of highways, and none of the 5 new highways became a
worse false alarm than the old top two -- consistent with the model
previously overfitting to the specific NO2/SO2 signature of a small,
geographically clustered highway sample rather than learning a
general highway-vs-plant boundary. Steel plants stayed roughly flat
(Bhilai 0.59->0.55, Jamshedpur 0.54->0.51 vs. Week 5's 0.52/0.50 --
within the noise expected from a re-split/re-train on a small dataset).

NEW WATCH ITEM: city_Kolkata is now the 3rd-highest false alarm
(0.42), ahead of most highways. Not urgent (below the 0.5 threshold),
but worth tracking if a future week revisits city false alarms.
