# Project Status & Next Steps (as of Week 11)

This document consolidates the project's history and remaining work across both tracks. It complements `README.md` (setup/usage) and `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` (the original research roadmap, written after Week 7) with a running "where we are now" view, since that synthesis previously required reading all of `WEEK2_LOG.txt`–`WEEK11_LOG.txt` together.

## Context

The project has two parallel tracks estimating CO2 emissions from Indian coal power plants using satellite data:

- **Track A (plant detector):** a CNN classifying satellite tiles as "power plant" vs. not, using NO2/SO2/VIIRS channels.
- **Track B (CO2 estimation):** a physics-based mass-balance (IME) method estimating tons CO2/yr directly from OCO-3 XCO2 soundings.

`RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §14 laid out a 7-step roadmap to turn this into a research contribution. This document tracks progress against that roadmap and records unplanned but important findings discovered along the way.

## Step-by-step history

### Weeks 2–5 — Track A detector, incremental channel fusion
- Week 2: NO2-only baseline, 91.2% accuracy (easy negatives) — later shown to really be a "concentrated combustion detector," not plant-specific.
- Week 3: added hard negatives (cities, steel plants, highways) → accuracy dropped to 77.1%, exposing the confound.
- Week 4: added SO2 channel → 79.2%, fixed city false alarms but not steel plants (which also emit SO2).
- Week 5: added VIIRS thermal → tied at 79.2%, reduced steel-plant false alarms specifically but highways became relatively more confusable.

### Weeks 6–8 — Track B physics pipeline + uncertainty
- Week 6: built `physics_gaussian.py`, an IME mass-balance estimator. Vindhyachal (44.6 Mt/yr) and Sasan (37.2 Mt/yr) landed in plausible range; Tirora too low (thin coverage); Mundra skipped (57 soundings only).
- Week 7: expanded highway hard-negatives (5→10), first Track A accuracy improvement since Week 4 (81.2%). Flagged the tile-level train/test split as a leakage risk (fixed later, Week 11).
- Week 8: per-overpass wind matching + uncertainty propagation into `physics_gaussian.py` (roadmap step 1) — replaced single annual-mean wind with per-overpass ERA5 matching, added checkpointing for long-running OCO-3 scans.

### Week 9 — facility-set expansion (roadmap step 2)
- Extended candidate list from 5 to 20 plants (`pick_plants.py`), generalized `process_plant.py` to read from a CSV registry instead of hardcoded plants.
- Processed 5 new plants (Talcher, Rihand, Sipat, ChandrapurCoal, Anpara): went from 4→9 total plants processed, 2/4→7/9 producing a plausible estimate.
- Measured real cost: ~50–65 min/plant (network-bound OCO-3 scan) — a documented constraint on how far facility expansion can go in one session. 10 candidates remain unprocessed.

### Week 10 — activity signal, reliability check, Climate TRACE benchmark, Talcher diagnosis
- Roadmap step 3: extracted an activity-probability signal from Track A's existing 3-channel CNN for all 9 Track B facilities. Broadly tracked the physics-based Q estimate.
- Roadmap step 4 (scoped down): tested whether activity signal / wind alignment / metadata predicts `physics_gaussian.py`'s own uncertainty. Negative result — best single-feature correlation didn't survive leave-one-out CV at N=7, reinforcing that more facilities are needed.
- Roadmap step 6: pulled Climate TRACE India power-sector CO2 data, matched to all 9 facilities. 5/7 facilities with a Track B estimate fell within stated uncertainty. Two misses: Sasan overestimated (2.13x), Talcher underestimated (0.50x) despite having the tightest interval of the session.
- Unplanned: diagnosed why Talcher was both most-confident and most-wrong. Root cause: thin CO2 signal-to-noise ratio (0.18 vs. 1.27 for a well-bracketed plant) and high sensitivity to the background-annulus definition (IME swings 20% across reasonable background choices) — a structural uncertainty source the model wasn't accounting for.

### Same-day follow-up — built a general leave-one-facility-out CV harness for Track A (roadmap step 5)
- Wrote `lofo_track_a.py`: previously step 5 was only satisfied ad hoc by Week 11's single random 80/20 facility-level split (`facility_level_split()` in `train_3channel.py`), which only ever tests generalization on whichever handful of facilities land in that one draw (4 plant facilities for the "mixed" run). This harness instead holds out each plant facility in turn, training a fresh model on the rest each time (21 folds total — Mundra's two units, `MUNDRA_TPP`/`MUNDRA_UMPP`, are separate groups in `data/top5_plants.csv`, covering the 20 Track B facilities), and measures recall on that facility's own tiles.
- **Result: the single-split 88% recall figure was not representative — true exhaustive LOFO recall is much lower and highly variable.** Unweighted mean recall across all 21 folds: **47.2%** (tile-weighted: 48.7%), essentially a coin flip on average.
  - Best-generalizing (recall ≥0.83): Anpara, Korba, Rihand, Talcher, Vindhyachal, Sasan.
  - Worst-generalizing (recall ≤0.08): Kahalgaon, Kudgi, Mouda, Tirora, Chhabra — the detector essentially never recognizes these as plants when excluded from training.
  - This is a materially more honest number for the paper than the 88% recall reported from the single random facility-split test set, which happened to draw an easier-than-typical subset of facilities. Full per-facility results in `data/lofo_track_a_results.json`.

### Same-day follow-up — what predicts LOFO recall? (`lofo_recall_correlates.py`)
- Answered the open question above: joined `lofo_track_a.py`'s per-facility recall (Mundra's two units averaged, tile-weighted, into one row) against `plant_results.json`/`candidate_plants.csv`/`activity_signals.json` features, N=20, same single-feature-LOO-CV method as `reliability_model.py`.
- **Result: `activity_prob_mean` (the standard, non-held-out model's own confidence) strongly predicts whether a held-out model recognizes the facility at all — r=+0.903, LOO R²=0.783, MAE=0.149.** `n_soundings` is a weaker secondary signal (r=+0.596). Interpretation: facilities with a weak/ambiguous NO2+SO2+VIIRS combustion signature (low activity_prob_mean even for a model that HAS seen every facility) are exactly the ones a held-out model fails hardest on — i.e. LOFO failure looks driven by intrinsic signal strength/distinctiveness per facility, not by memorization of specific sites. Caveat: `activity_prob_mean` and `lofo_recall` both derive from the same detector architecture/tile set, so this correlation says "signal clarity predicts generalizability," not two fully independent measurements.
- Fixed a silent bug the analysis surfaced: `co2_enhancement_ppm` is null for the 3 facilities `physics_gaussian.py` excludes (Mundra, Simhadri, Sipat — 0 near-plant soundings), which made its correlation compute as `NaN` via plain `np.corrcoef`; switched to pairwise deletion (N=17 for that one feature) so it reports a real value (r=−0.184) instead of silently failing.
- Full feature table and LOO predictions in `data/lofo_recall_correlates.json`.

### Same-day follow-up — root-caused the negative-CO2-enhancement anomaly (ShriSingajiMalwa, Koradi, Tamnar)
- Wrote `diagnose_negative_enhancement.py`, reusing `diagnose_talcher.py`'s `near_bg_stats`/`bg_definition_sensitivity` functions plus a new statistical-significance check: is the near-minus-background difference distinguishable from zero given the standard error of that difference (near/background sample sizes and variance), or is the negative sign just sampling noise landing on the wrong side of zero?
- **Result: 2 of 3 facilities are not really "negative enhancement" at all.** Koradi (z=−1.58) and Tamnar (z=−1.33) are both statistically consistent with zero at 2σ — the honest characterization is "no detectable enhancement given available signal-to-noise," not a real negative signal. This also resolves the apparent contradiction with the Talcher-derived heuristic: Tamnar's good wind/CO2 alignment (16°, the best of the three) says its NO2 plume geometry is plausible, but wind alignment and CO2 signal-to-noise are computed independently — the two diagnostics are orthogonal, not in tension.
- **ShriSingajiMalwa is different: its negative enhancement (−1.052 ppm, z=−5.32) is statistically significant, not noise.** Follow-up (`diagnose_shrisingajimalwa.py`) resolved this too. All 59 near-plant soundings are from a single month (January) — likely OCO-3 swath geometry only crossing the tight near-plant circle on some overpasses — while the background ring also draws from April/May (mean XCO2 ~415.6–416.5 ppm, ~4 ppm higher than January's ~412 ppm baseline for reasons unrelated to the plant) and October. **Restricting the comparison to the one month both zones actually share (January) flips the sign: near=412.321 vs bg=412.029 ppm, a small but positive +0.292 ppm difference (z=2.13)** — consistent with a real, if weak, plant signal, not a negative anomaly. The original "negative enhancement" was a seasonal-sampling-imbalance artifact (comparing January-only near-plant data against a January/April/May/October blended background), not a real negative CO2 signal, a pipeline bug, or evidence against the plant emitting. **Note for future work: `physics_gaussian.py`'s near/background comparison does not currently stratify by month, so this failure mode could recur for any facility with uneven near/background month coverage** — worth a general fix, not just a one-off explanation, if it recurs.
- All three facilities show elevated background-definition sensitivity relative to the well-bracketed comparison facility (Rihand: 4% IME-proxy range vs. ShriSingajiMalwa 45%, Tamnar 37%, Koradi 19%), consistent with the same thin-signal fragility pattern the Talcher diagnosis first identified — not a new failure mode. Full stats in `data/negative_enhancement_diagnosis.json` and `data/shrisingajimalwa_investigation.json`.
- **All three previously-flagged negative-enhancement facilities are now resolved** — the negative-CO2-enhancement anomaly item is closed, though the general month-stratification gap it surfaced in `physics_gaussian.py` remains a worthwhile hardening item.
