WEEK 10 — DONE (activity signal, reliability check, Climate TRACE benchmark,
                 Talcher diagnosis)

- Continuation of the same session as Week 9's facility-set expansion.
  RESEARCH_PLAN.md's roadmap (Sec 14) calls for steps 3 (activity-signal
  extraction), 4 (correction model), and 6 (Climate TRACE benchmark) next.
  This week worked through all three, plus an unplanned but necessary
  fourth piece: diagnosing the one facility whose Climate TRACE comparison
  came back genuinely wrong.

- Roadmap step 3 -- activity signal (export_facility_tiles.py,
  extract_activity_signal.py): reused Track A's already-trained 3-channel
  CNN (detector3_2ch_mixed.pt) as-is, per RESEARCH_PLAN.md Sec 15's
  explicit instruction not to retrain it yet. Exported 2020-only NO2/SO2/
  VIIRS tiles for the 5 facilities not already covered by Track A's
  original 5-plant export (180 tiles, much faster than the OCO-3 scans --
  small array downloads, not large granule files). Extracted a per-
  facility activity probability + 64-dim embedding for all 9 Track B
  facilities, carefully reproducing train_3channel.py's per-channel
  normalization (which is computed at train time but never saved to disk
  with the checkpoint -- reconstructed by reloading the original training
  tiles and recomputing the same mean/std). Result: the activity signal
  broadly tracks the physics-based Q estimate (the 4 highest-activity
  facilities are also the 4 highest-emission estimates), and separately
  flagged ChandrapurCoal (low activity signal despite a moderate Q
  estimate) as a plant whose IME result might be less trustworthy --
  a flag that the Climate TRACE comparison later complicated rather than
  confirmed (see below).

- Roadmap step 4 -- correction model, scoped down (reliability_model.py):
  RESEARCH_PLAN.md describes a "correction model" that outputs a
  corrected Q_final, but that needs a training target we don't have --
  Climate TRACE wasn't pulled yet at this point in the week, and
  RESEARCH_PLAN.md Sec 7 says it must not be used as a training label
  regardless. Scoped the question down to something answerable now: does
  the activity signal, wind/CO2 alignment, or facility metadata predict
  physics_gaussian.py's own self-reported q_rel_std? Not circular --
  q_rel_std is derived purely from ERA5 wind variance and IME bootstrap
  noise, independent of the candidate features. Result: a real negative
  finding. Best single-feature correlation (activity_prob_std, r=-0.62)
  does not survive leave-one-out CV at N=7 (LOO R^2 = -1.22, worse than
  predicting the mean). Documents, rather than papers over, that 7
  facilities is too few for this kind of modeling -- reinforces
  RESEARCH_PLAN.md's original argument for expanding past the current set.

- Roadmap step 6 -- Climate TRACE benchmark (pull_climate_trace.py):
  API endpoint discovered empirically (api.climatetrace.org/v6/assets,
  found via the site's beta API reference and verified live with curl
  before writing any code -- not guessed). Pulled India power-sector CO2
  for all 9 Track B facilities, matched by lat/lon centroid (exact
  matches, e.g. Vindhyachal's Climate TRACE centroid is identical to our
  own coordinates). Year caveat: Climate TRACE's asset API has no 2020
  coverage (year=2020 returns zero assets) -- used 2021, the nearest
  available year, against Track B's 2020 estimates. Result: 5/7 facilities
  with a Track B estimate have Climate TRACE's value within our stated
  uncertainty interval -- a reasonable hit rate given how wide those
  intervals are (+/-45-74% for most plants). The two misses are the
  interesting part: Sasan overestimates (2.13x Climate TRACE) and Talcher
  underestimates (0.50x) despite having the TIGHTEST, most confident
  interval of any plant this session (+/-20%) -- confidence and
  correctness pulled apart on exactly the plant that looked most solid.
  ChandrapurCoal, flagged earlier via the activity signal as our least-
  trustworthy estimate, actually has the closest ratio to 1.0 (0.81x) --
  a complication for that narrative, not confirmation of it. Framed
  throughout as benchmark-vs-benchmark, not ground truth, per
  RESEARCH_PLAN.md Sec 10/12 -- Climate TRACE's own accuracy on Indian
  plants is itself unvalidated.

- Talcher diagnosis (diagnose_talcher.py), prompted by asking "why is the
  most confident estimate also the most wrong":
  1. Ruled out a plant-identity mismatch -- Climate TRACE's match distance
     is 0.0011 deg (~120m), same 3000MW plant, same coordinates.
  2. Ruled out the 2020-vs-2021 year offset as the primary cause. Climate
     TRACE's own multi-year trend for this exact asset (14.24M in 2021 ->
     17.04M in 2024, 6.2% CAGR) extrapolates back to ~13.4M for 2020 --
     still a 1.9x gap versus our 7.16M, so the year mismatch explains only
     a modest fraction of the discrepancy, not most of it.
  3. Found the real cause: Talcher's raw CO2 enhancement (near-plant mean
     minus background mean) is tiny relative to background noise --
     signal-to-noise ratio 0.18, versus 1.27 for Rihand (a well-bracketed
     comparison plant). Only 51.6% of near-plant soundings exceed the
     background mean at all (a near coin-flip), versus 75.6% for Rihand.
  4. Directly measured what physics_gaussian.py's docstring already warns
     about but doesn't quantify: sensitivity to the background-annulus
     definition. Testing 5 reasonable background-radius choices, Talcher's
     IME estimate swings 20% of its default value; Rihand's swings only
     4%. Talcher's tight +/-20% reported uncertainty (wind + IME-sampling
     noise only) doesn't include this structural sensitivity at all, so
     the interval understates the real uncertainty specifically for this
     plant.
  5. One point in favor of the signal being real rather than pure noise:
     Talcher has the best wind/CO2 offset alignment of any plant this
     session (25 deg, from Week 9) -- the faint pattern detected is at
     least directionally consistent with wind-driven transport.
  Conclusion: not a code bug, not a plant mismatch, and only partly a year
  effect. A genuine case where the IME method's implicit assumption
  (background cleanly separable from signal) breaks down for a plant with
  a thin OCO-3 CO2 signature, and the uncertainty model's blind spot
  (background-definition structural uncertainty, already documented in
  physics_gaussian.py's own docstring since Week 8) turns out to matter
  most exactly where it's least visible -- a facility whose OTHER
  diagnostics (uncertainty band, wind alignment) look fine.

NEXT: the background-definition-sensitivity check in diagnose_talcher.py
is currently a one-off diagnostic, not a per-plant automated flag. Per
RESEARCH_PLAN.md's priority order (scientific validity first), a natural
follow-up is running this same sensitivity test across all 9 facilities
and folding a background-sensitivity term into physics_gaussian.py's
reported uncertainty, rather than leaving it as a manual investigation
triggered only when a plant looks suspicious after the fact. Facility-set
expansion (10 candidates remaining from Week 9's shortlist) and
retrying the reliability-model exercise at higher N both remain open,
per Week 9's NEXT section.
